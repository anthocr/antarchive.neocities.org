# We Are The Ants (2016)
#lgbt #novel #coming-of-age #mental-health

Author: Shaun David Hutchinson
### Rating
2/5
## Synopsis
We follow Henry Denton in this coming of age novel set in a small town. We soon learn that Henry is periodically abducted by aliens that have presented him with the decision of the fate of their world. We follow his story as he comes to terms with his boyfriend's suicide and the subsequent descent into depression and sometimes self destructive behaviors. 

## Thoughts
This novel really takes you for a trip. I really enjoyed the hook with Henry being abducted by aliens immediately. From Henry's descriptions these '*sluggers*' as he calls them don't follow the rules that we know which really inrigued me. Hearing him describe their disturbing form with minonitiny added to his characterization as a depressed and detached teenager. We soon find out Henry has many challenges in his life to a point where for me it feels somewhat unrealistic.

My main problem with this book is the lengths the author goes to torture our protaginist. Henry has a troubled home life with jis brother beating the crap out of him at one point and his mother not being present most of the time. He goes to school and is abused, almost raped, and sexually assaulted. He rarely finds any solace in friends, activities, or anything else in his life. The events tart to feel very gratuitous and drawn out with certain sections makinf me feel sick and just wondering when the story was going to take a more positive note.

Another big issue I had with this book is the insatisfying conclusion. **Heavy spoilers** are incoming if you still want to read this book. At the end of the book Henry is shipped to a mental facility (as he should be with all the shit the author put him through). He doesn't really have anyhting to be grateful for to not end the world, besides his new boyfriend Diego. This felt extremely unsatisfying to me especially since the whole narrative of the book is built to give Henry somethign to care about to **not** end the world (or having him choose to end the world anyways but that would be more satisfying in my opinion). On top of this they straight up impy that the **aliens might not have been real at all!** This is just ridiculous to me since this would imply that Henry is not a reliable narrator at all. There are some stories that utilize an unrelaible narrator effectively but this is not one of those stories. 

Henry's behavior also makes him pretty unlikable as our protaganist. His behavior can be empathized with from my personal experience, however I believe that most readers would nto be able to without having gone through something similar. He actively sabotages anything with potential for good in his life and treats most characters awful (even the few characters that like him).

That being said, there are some good messages and characters in this book. I believe that most of the characters including Henry are extemely well written. Henry's family dynamic was my favorite part of this book displaying how trauma can effect the family unit in different ways. Mental illness was depicted in a way that was realistic and mostly tasteful. However, if you struggle with mental health I may reccomend to skip this one. There are also many garpahic depictions of subjects like SA and suicide.
